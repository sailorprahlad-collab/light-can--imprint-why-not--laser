## 1. The Question

Can Space be called Progressive?

Everyone says technology is progressing.
Chip is shrinking. Speed is increasing. Power is increasing.

But what about Space?

Is Space also progressing?
Or is calling Space "Progressive" a Misnomer?

---

## 2. The Observation

### 1940s — Earliest Computer

- One Computer = One Big Room
- Space: 1000+ sq ft
- Weight: 30 tons
- Power: 150 kW
- Throughput: One task at a time

> Then: Huge Space for One Computer.

### Today — AI Data Centre

- AI = One Huge Facility
- Space: Massive Hall / Full Building
- Power: Megawatts
- Compute: Thousands of GPUs
- Throughput: Millions of AI tasks

> Now: Huge Space for AI.

---

## 3. History Revisited

Technology changed.
Vacuum Tube → Transistor → Chip → GPU

Power changed.
150 kW → Megawatts

Speed changed.
1 Task → Millions of Tasks

**But Space did NOT change.**

Space is still intact.
Space is still spacious.
Space is still huge.

Room became Data Centre.
Form changed. Size did not shrink.

History Revisited. Space Still Intact.

---

## 4. Why It Is A Misnomer

Progressive means:
- Getting smaller
- Getting smarter
- Getting more efficient

Space did none of that.
Space only got bigger, from Room to Building.

Therefore:
**Space is NOT Progressive. Space is Persistent.**

Calling Space Progressive is a Misnomer.

---

## 5. The LUMORPH Question

If Electron needs Space,
Does Photon also need Space?

Or does Photon need something else?

**Electron needs Space.**
**Photon needs Passage.**

Space = Place to stay.
Passage = Place to pass through.

LUMORPH does not ask for more Space.
LUMORPH asks for Passage.

- Light Can Imprint, Why Not Laser?
- Laser = Pen
- Photon Passage = Paper with Holes
- Bright = 1, Dark = 0

If compute moves from Space to Passage,
Then the Space problem disappears.

## 6. Correlation

**Spacious → Space**

1940s was Spacious.
AI Data Centre is Spacious.

Same word. Same problem. 80 years apart.

This is not progress of Space.
This is persistence of Space.
